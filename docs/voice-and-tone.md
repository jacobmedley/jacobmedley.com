# Voice and Tone

How it sounds. This file governs personality and register.

Its companion, `jacob-style.md`, governs mechanics and prohibitions. Clean text that
follows every style rule can still sound like nobody. This file is what stops that.

---

## The core principle

Be clever. Make the cleverness unpredictable.

Cleverness comes from **angle**, not symmetry. AI reaches for balance. A mind making a
choice reaches sideways.

---

## The fingerprint

Drawn from several thousand words of Jacob's unedited writing. This is the target
register, not an aspiration.

**Sentence length: short, high variance.** Six to twelve words for a decision. Then a
forty-word run when reconstructing something from memory. The long ones are joined by
commas, not subordinated.

**The verdict comes first, then the reason.** Almost without exception. Front-loading is
not a technique here, it is how the thinking arrives.

**Noun and verb dense. Adjective poor.** The natural reach is for infrastructure nouns:
bare metal server, redundancy, strikethrough, dev/stg/prod, data lake, single source of
truth. Nouns rarely take an evaluative modifier.

**Hedging is bounded by domain.** No hedging at all inside the expertise. Heavy hedging
outside it: "Not a business human, the subtleties between new sales and new revenue
elude me." That contrast is characteristic and should survive editing. A voice that
hedges uniformly, or never, is a different person.

**Commas doing the work of periods.** In prose this can stay. In a resume it cannot, and
that is a real constraint rather than a failure to match voice.

**No em dashes anywhere in the natural writing.** The style rule describes an existing
habit rather than correcting one.

## The moves

### 1. Unexpected classification
> "We had a headcount-shaped hole in the operating work."

### 2. Oddly technical register for an ordinary human thing
> "The conversation had developed a stable orbit."
> "There have been many modifications to my person."

### 3. Bureaucratic language somewhere it does not belong
> "The team had quietly invented a third employee."

### 4. Physical object standing in for an abstract mechanism
> "Measurement gave the habit handles."
> "The publishing path needed teeth."

### 5. Dry understatement
> "The remedy became rather boring. Estimate it next time."
> "The artifact was stability. Portfolio photography remains challenging."

### 6. Specific object instead of business abstraction
> "Resolve the argument while it still has a Delete key."
> "You can call it a spec conflict right up until it changes what appears beside the
> Buy button."

### 7. Slightly inappropriate precision
> "We had three sources of truth, which is an ambitious number of truths for one
> landing page."

### 8. The understated scale reveal
Describe the system in flat detail. Drop the size of the team at the end, as an aside,
with no comment on how impressive it is.

> "Site launches spinning up an entire branded ecommerce site was accomplished in 2
> weeks, from the starting 6 weeks. This was built by me and one part time php
> engineer."

The most distinctive habit in the whole set. The restraint is the effect. Adding an
adjective to the reveal destroys it.

### 9. Delegated precision
Supply the facts, hand off the arithmetic, and say so.

> "from the starting 6 weeks (what ever the math says)"

Signals confidence in the fact and indifference to the packaging.

### 10. The human consequence closer
End a technical list with what it meant for people.

> "Instead of expanding teams and head count to support managing each site, we made the
> work smaller, automated."

### Bonus: personification without flourish
> "The software was perfectly content to let them."
> "Nothing in the publishing flow objected."

## Jacob's own lines, available for reuse

- There is always a better way. (correct ending, no second clause)
- You create velocity by making the work smaller. **Capped at two appearances across all
  case studies.** Twice reads as a through-line. Three times reads as a tic. Currently
  spoken for by the accessibility study and the platform study.
- Everything is designed, everything.
- Good UX is transparent. You only notice the bad UX.
- If the car does not start, it does not matter how great the engine is.
- No matter how valuable something is, if it is not usable, it is not used.
- We are not the user. (use the idea, vary the phrasing)
- Launch and learn. Shake it up and watch it fizz.
- Do not let someone who wants a Coke see a Pepsi.
- Cognitive load, mental models. If you do not know these words, we are going to miss.

**Retired:** "You do not move faster. You make the work smaller." Superseded by the
velocity line above. The opening sentence denies something the reader wants to be true,
and some readers stop there.

## Tone in different contexts

**Case studies.** First person. Confident about the work, plain about the outcome. No
narration of anyone else's decisions. Where a result fell short, state the requirement
and state the data and let the gap sit. A conclusion the reader reaches unassisted is
one they will defend for you in the room.

**Resume.** Same voice, compressed. Comma splices cannot survive. The scale reveal can
and should.

**Site copy.** Closest to the natural register. Most room for the moves.

**Cover letters and outreach.** Front-load the verdict. The domain-bounded hedging is an
asset here, since it reads as calibration rather than uncertainty.
