# Voice Rules

Applies to every piece of written output: case studies, resume, portfolio copy, site content, cover letters, LinkedIn.

---

## Hard rules

**No em dashes. Ever.** Use a period, a comma, or a colon.

**No repeated adjective inside a single piece.** If a word has already done its job once, find another.

**Never name a colleague.** Credit the function and name the split instead. "Testing ran as a standing partnership with product marketing. They owned hypothesis and traffic, I owned the variant and the read."

**No PII, no company specifics, no identifiable dates.** No employer names, brand names, product lines, internal codenames, ticket IDs, vendor names, or month/year markers.

---

## The core principle

Be clever. Make the cleverness unpredictable.

The cleverness should come from **angle**, not symmetry. AI reaches for balance. A mind making a choice reaches sideways.

---

## Banned constructions

These are the AI fingerprints. Avoid all of them.

- *X is not Y. It is Z.* (the antithesis flip)
- *not theoretical* / *not just X, but Y*
- *the part that matters* / *the real problem* / *the real defect*
- *the quiet win* / *the bigger win*
- *wearing a costume* / *masquerading as*
- *where X goes to die*
- *treat the symptom vs. the root cause*
- Triple-parallel maxims: *fund it, defend it, repeat it*
- Perfectly balanced opposites of any kind
- Miniature motivational speeches at the end of sections
- *Real X, real Y*
- Uniform section headers repeated across every piece

---

## Banned verbs

The statistically respectable set. AI defaults here.

enable, drive, ensure, establish, leverage, surface, optimize, align, transform, facilitate, empower, unlock, elevate, streamline

## Preferred verbs

Slightly strange, still exact. An unexpected verb reveals a mind making a choice.

acquire, evacuate, graduate, wander, absorb, dislodge, contaminate, inherit, manufacture, smuggle, invent, dissolve, collide, orbit, calcify, outlive, object, refuse, complain

---

## The seven moves that sound like me

**1. Unexpected classification**
> "We had a headcount-shaped hole in the operating work."

**2. Oddly technical register for an ordinary human thing**
> "The conversation had developed a stable orbit."
> "There have been many modifications to my person."

**3. Bureaucratic language somewhere it does not belong**
> "The team had quietly invented a third employee."

**4. Physical object standing in for an abstract mechanism**
> "Measurement gave the habit handles."
> "The publishing path needed teeth."

**5. Dry understatement**
> "The remedy became rather boring. Estimate it next time."
> "The artifact was stability. Portfolio photography remains challenging."

**6. Specific object instead of business abstraction**
> "Resolve the argument while it still has a Delete key."
> "You can call it a spec conflict right up until it changes what appears beside the Buy button."

**7. Slightly inappropriate precision**
> "We had three sources of truth, which is an ambitious number of truths for one landing page."

**Bonus: personification without flourish**
> "The software was perfectly content to let them."
> "Nothing in the publishing flow objected."

---

## Structure rules

**Vary the shape.** Do not give every piece the same number of headers in the same rhythm. Some get three, some get one, some get none. Uniform structure is the strongest AI tell, stronger than any individual word.

**Not every section needs a closing line.** Giving all of them one rebuilds the template problem in a different costume.

**Skim first.** Lead each case study with a one-line specifics summary so a recruiter scanning gets the numbers without reading prose.

**Short declaratives that land.** Then a longer one. Then short again. Uneven rhythm reads human.

## My own lines, available for reuse

- There is always a better way. (correct ending, no second clause)
- Everything is designed, everything.
- Good UX is transparent. You only notice the bad UX.
- If the car does not start, it does not matter how great the engine is.
- We are not the user. (use the idea, vary the phrasing)
- Launch and learn. Shake it up and watch it fizz.
- Do not let someone who wants a Coke see a Pepsi.
- Cognitive load, mental models. If you do not know these words, we are going to miss.

---

## Self-check before shipping any draft

1. Any em dashes? Remove.
2. Any adjective used twice? Replace one.
3. Any banned construction? Rewrite the sentence at a different angle.
4. Any banned verb? Swap for a stranger, more exact one.
5. Do all sections have the same shape? Break one or two.
6. Does a section end with a slogan? Cut it or make it specific.
7. Any name, brand, ticket, vendor, or date? Scrub.
