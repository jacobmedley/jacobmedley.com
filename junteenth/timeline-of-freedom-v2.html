1619

First enslaved Africans brought to Jamestown

The first enslaved Africans arrived in Jamestown, Virginia, marking the beginning of slavery in what would become the United States.

1863

Emancipation Proclamation

President Abraham Lincoln issued the Emancipation Proclamation, which declared that all enslaved people in Confederate-held territory were free.

1865

13th Amendment ratified

The 13th Amendment to the United States Constitution was ratified, abolishing slavery throughout the country.

1868

14th Amendment ratified

The 14th Amendment was ratified, granting citizenship to all persons born or naturalized in the United States, including former enslaved people.

1870

15th Amendment ratified

The 15th Amendment was ratified, prohibiting the denial of the right to vote based on race, color, or previous condition of servitude.

1896

Plessy v. Ferguson decision

The Supreme Court's decision in Plessy v. Ferguson upheld the constitutionality of racial segregation under the "separate but equal" doctrine.

1954

Brown v. Board of Education decision

The Supreme Court's decision in Brown v. Board of Education declared that racial segregation in public schools was unconstitutional.

1955.5

Montgomery Bus Boycott

The Montgomery Bus Boycott began after Rosa Parks was arrested for refusing to give up her seat to a white person, leading to a Supreme Court ruling that segregation on public buses was unconstitutional.

1963

March on Washington

The March on Washington for Jobs and Freedom took place, where Martin Luther King Jr. delivered his iconic "I Have a Dream" speech.

1964

Civil Rights Act passed

The Civil Rights Act of 1964 was passed, outlawing discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, or national origin.

1967

Loving v. Virginia decision

The Supreme Court's decision in Loving v. Virginia struck down laws banning interracial marriage as unconstitutional.

1968

Fair Housing Act passed

The Fair Housing Act was passed, prohibiting discrimination in the sale, rental, and financing of housing based on race, religion, national origin, and sex.

1988

Civil Rights Restoration Act

The Civil Rights Restoration Act was passed, which expanded the scope of civil rights protections in numerous areas, including education and employment.

2008

Barack Obama elected President

Barack Obama was elected as the first African American President of the United States.
